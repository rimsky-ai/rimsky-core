# The two record shapes, their emission sites, the required outcome, hashed identities, and the terminal-kind family

Unsupported, on the terminal-kind family and the two emission-exclusion claims. What holds: both record kinds are append-only by construction, since the table interface exposes insert, reads, and a retention delete and no update. Leaf-run records are written from the per-terminal handlers in the runtime, claim-terminal records from the single forensics emit site inside the unified terminal-decision engine, so commit, natural abandon, and both force-cancelled paths land in one shape. The outcome value is required and the writer rejects an empty one with an error naming the three legal values, so an abandon path that forgets to set it cannot produce a record that reads as committed. Identities are hashed rather than carried: held-claim citations carry a claim-scope hash, and the record also carries params and attribute-bag hashes, with an unassigned open-metadata field present as described. The claim-terminal record carries the terminating supervisor's identity, the producer name, the substrate version, the disposition outcome, and a separate cause discriminator distinguishing the sibling-cancel walker from the descendant-cancel descent. Sub-graph callers do produce two leaf-run records per dispatch, one at the internal-cascade-fire site and one at the post-aggregation terminal, and the external-receiver subscriber maps each leaf-run record to one completion event. What is not carried: the terminal-kind discriminator is described as a small closed family covering four cases — success, park, error, and the sub-graph caller's internal-cascade-fire emission. Enumerating the emission sites across the runtime and scheduler yields six distinct values in use: the four named ones plus a handler-pass value written whenever an error policy's pass disposition transitions a run, and a pure-cascade value written by the scheduler's own settlement sweep. The last two also refute two exclusion claims the concept makes. A pre-dispatch acquisition failure resolved by a pass disposition does emit a leaf-run record — the acquire-phase handlers route through the same shared error-policy path, which transitions the stale run and emits under the handler-pass discriminator. And pure-cascade nodes, named as pass-throughs that emit nothing, are exactly what the scheduler's sweep writes a record for.
