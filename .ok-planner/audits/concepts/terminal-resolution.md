# The four-stage spine from executor outcome to producer verb and claim-handle row

Unsupported, on the carve-out invariant. Most of the spine is there as described: the outcome reader maps the wire's four variants to completion, error, park and async-accepted and synthesizes an infra kind for a nil or unrecognized outcome; the terminal-application step routes completion, error, infra and park to per-kind handlers and increments a per-class verdict counter, with park settling on a fixed resolution and retaining its claims rather than reaching lock release; retry loops in place without releasing locks while give-up, pass and release-and-requeue all reach it; the infra path retries below a per-node cap and gives up at it; the async outcome re-enters through the callback path, which produces an ordinary completion, error or park event. Lock release deletes a named lock's handle row claimant-guarded and routes claims into one resolution engine whose three sources are active-terminal, held-terminal and ownership-bail, the last deleting the row and emitting no signal; that engine records the disposition, enqueues the verb on the outbox and resolves the row claimant-guarded in one transaction. The outbox itself matches the text: rows carry no foreign keys so they outlive the instance, delivery is at-least-once with clock-injected exponential backoff, ordering is per producer-and-scope with head-of-line blocking, a new open first drains the conflicting scope's undelivered terminals through the same delivery path, a commit response's version identifier and fan-out child metadata are applied after delivery, and undelivered counts are exposed on an admin diagnostics route. The administrative-kill fallback behaves exactly as claimed, recording the disposition without a verb when the producer cannot be resolved or the engine call fails. The invariant that fails is the one naming the acquire-unavailable handler as the single carve-out outside the engine: the same bypass — enqueue an Abandon for partially-opened claims with no row to delete — is taken from seven call sites covering at least six conditions, namely acquire-unavailable, acquire-producer-error, fan-out partition-request substitution failure, lock-spec substitution failure, restamp-CAS loss, a generic acquisition error, and the try-acquire rollback sentinel. The project's own decision on this point already names two carve-outs rather than one, so the concept understates its own design. A second, smaller inaccuracy: the retry cap does not short-circuit before policy lookup on the error path — the policy is looked up first and the cap applies only where the resolved action is retry — though the pass action does end the dispatch without consulting the cap as the text says, and the infra path's cap check does precede everything.
