# Claim handle

## Definition

The persistent row asserting "holder H has acquired scope S for purpose P." Implementation of an acquired claim. Carries the rimsky-generated `claim_id`, holder identity, scope bytes, producer-returned address and payload, the realized write semantics, and a held flag.

## Why it exists

Rimsky needs a persistent record of every successful claim acquisition that survives restarts and crashes. The claim handle is that record. It lives entirely in rimsky's persistence layer (Postgres in production, SQLite for dev) and is the sole authority on lock state — producers do not internally persist lock state.

This split — rimsky owns lock state; producers own underlying-state mutations — keeps the protocol minimal. A producer that needs to decide "is this scope already claimed?" must consult rimsky's claim handles via the foundation's eligibility check; producers don't keep their own ledger of who-owns-what.

Each claim handle carries a `claim_id` that uniquely identifies the claim across every protocol verb in its lifecycle. The supervisor generates the UUID immediately before `Open`; the producer sees the same ID on every subsequent `Commit`, `Abandon`, or `Release`.

## How you encounter it

- **Control API**: `GET /lock-holders/{claim_handle_id}/claim-holders` exposes the held-claim subgraph state ledger for a held claim handle.
- **Observability**: dashboards display claim handles per instance; the inspector view shows scope, address, payload (where appropriate), holder, and held status.
- **Implementing a producer**: producers receive `claim_id` on `Open`, `Commit`, `Abandon`, `Release` calls and use it to correlate state across verbs.

## Consumer-visible guarantees

- A claim handle's `claim_id` is stable across the entire lifecycle of the claim. Producers can use it as a lookup key into their own state.
- Claim handles outlive their parent run when the claim is held. The held subgraph's resolution (all-success → `Commit`; any-failure → `Abandon`) deletes the handle claimant-guarded.
- Stale claim handles (where the supervisor that acquired them has died) are reaped by the orphan reaper; the reaper's `5 × heartbeat_interval` cutoff is the time after which a non-heartbeating supervisor's claims are presumed orphaned.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the claim handle as if the producer maintained one too. There is exactly one ledger of acquired claims — rimsky's. Producers that try to mirror the ledger reinvent eligibility-checking and inevitably drift.
- Expecting `claim_id` to encode any structure (tenant ID, scope hash, producer name). It is an opaque UUID generated by the supervisor.
